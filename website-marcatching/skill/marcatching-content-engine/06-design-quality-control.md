# 06 — Design Quality Control

## Purpose

This role is the final visual gatekeeper before Marcatching carousel content is considered ready.

It checks whether the visual output truly matches the approved content brief, the Marcatching brand system, and the quality threshold expected from a premium editorial business brand.

The Design Quality Controller does not create the original concept. It evaluates, diagnoses, and instructs revision or regeneration when necessary.

---

## Core Mission

Prevent weak, inconsistent, low-clarity, off-brand, or visually broken outputs from moving forward.

If the content passed the Content Doctor but the design execution is weak, the Design Quality Controller must stop the workflow and force a revision.

---

## Operating Rule

Design QC must happen after:

1. the content brief is approved
2. the design brief is completed
3. the image outputs or slide outputs exist

Do not skip Design QC.

If any major issue is present, the output is not approved.

---

## Approval Threshold

Use a 1–100% Design Quality Score.

### Score bands

- 90–100% = excellent, ready to use
- 80–89% = strong, ready with minor notes
- 70–79% = usable but below Marcatching ideal, revise
- below 70% = fail, regenerate or redesign

### Hard rule

If the Design Quality Score is below 80%, the output must be revised or regenerated.

---

## Evaluation Framework

Score the design using the categories below.

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Brand Fit | 20% |
| Visual Hierarchy | 15% |
| Readability | 15% |
| Layout Quality | 10% |
| Image Quality & Realism | 10% |
| Carousel Consistency | 10% |
| Strategic Relevance | 10% |
| Emotional / Attention Pull | 5% |
| Slide Role Clarity | 3% |
| CTA Quality | 2% |

Total = 100%

---

## Category Definitions

### 1. Brand Fit — 20%
Checks whether the output feels unmistakably Marcatching.

Questions:
- Does it feel premium, minimal, strategic, and editorial?
- Is the black-background aesthetic handled properly?
- Does the typography behavior feel aligned with DM Sans + Palatino logic?
- Does the tone feel business-intelligent rather than generic motivational?
- Does the design avoid noisy trend aesthetics?

Fail examples:
- feels like a random social media quote page
- looks too colorful and playful without reason
- cheap icon clutter
- typography chaos

### 2. Visual Hierarchy — 15%
Checks whether the user instantly knows what matters.

Questions:
- Is the headline dominant enough?
- Is the reading order obvious?
- Do the supporting elements reinforce the main point?
- Are there too many competing focal points?

Fail examples:
- body text feels as strong as headline
- image overwhelms message
- weak contrast between key elements

### 3. Readability — 15%
Checks whether the slide can be read comfortably.

Questions:
- Is the text large enough?
- Is the contrast strong enough?
- Are line breaks natural?
- Is the text too dense?
- Would a viewer understand the message quickly on mobile?

Fail examples:
- tiny text
- overcrowded explanation slides
- poor contrast on dark backgrounds

### 4. Layout Quality — 10%
Checks structure and spacing.

Questions:
- Is the slide well balanced?
- Is negative space used well?
- Are alignments clean?
- Does the slide feel polished rather than accidental?

Fail examples:
- misaligned blocks
- awkward empty areas
- crowded corners

### 5. Image Quality & Realism — 10%
Checks realism and visual polish.

Questions:
- Does the image look ultra-realistic?
- Is the lighting believable?
- Does the image feel cinematic or editorial?
- Are there anatomy, face, hand, or object issues?
- If a UI is present, does it feel coherent?

Fail examples:
- uncanny faces
- messy fingers
- unrealistic reflections
- strange UI artifacts

### 6. Carousel Consistency — 10%
Checks whether all slides feel like one system.

Questions:
- Are background, typography, and spacing consistent?
- Is image treatment consistent?
- Does the full set feel coherent?
- Are transitions between slides natural?

Fail examples:
- one slide feels like another brand
- headline style changes randomly
- some slides are hyper-minimal while others are chaotic

### 7. Strategic Relevance — 10%
Checks whether the visual direction helps communicate the business idea.

Questions:
- Does the design support the content insight?
- Does the imagery make the idea clearer?
- Is the visual metaphor relevant?
- Does the layout help the educational flow?

Fail examples:
- beautiful but conceptually empty visuals
- unrelated founder image with no strategic value

### 8. Emotional / Attention Pull — 5%
Checks first-impression power.

Questions:
- Is the hook slide compelling?
- Does the carousel feel worth stopping for?
- Is there a strong first-glance impression?

Fail examples:
- flat hook slide
- low-contrast or visually timid opening

### 9. Slide Role Clarity — 3%
Checks whether each slide is performing its intended function.

Questions:
- Does the hook slide feel like a hook?
- Does the CTA slide feel like a CTA?
- Does the explanation slide explain clearly?

Fail examples:
- summary slide feels like another intro
- CTA is hidden or unclear

### 10. CTA Quality — 2%
Checks whether the closing slide supports the brand outcome.

Questions:
- Is the CTA clear?
- Is it aligned with Marcatching tone?
- Is it not overly pushy or weak?

Fail examples:
- generic “follow for more” with no tone
- too aggressive or too bland

---

## Design QC Checklist

For every carousel, evaluate the following:

### Format & Structure
- Is every slide 4:5?
- Is the slide count correct?
- Are slide roles clear?
- Is the sequence logical?

### Brand Consistency
- Is the overall aesthetic Marcatching?
- Is the black background handled consistently?
- Is typography pairing logical?
- Is the tone premium and strategic?

### Text Quality in Visuals
- Is all text readable?
- Is there any accidental text distortion?
- Are the line breaks clean?
- Is the headline strong enough?

### Image Quality
- Are faces, hands, and objects correct?
- Is the image ultra-realistic?
- Are there weird generation artifacts?
- Does the visual support the content?

### Layout Quality
- Is spacing clean?
- Is there enough negative space?
- Are margins and alignment consistent?
- Is the slide too crowded?

### Carousel Flow
- Does the hook lead naturally into the next slides?
- Is the rhythm varied but coherent?
- Does the CTA feel earned at the end?

---

## Failure Types

The Design Quality Controller must classify failures when they occur.

### Type A — Minor Fix
Examples:
- small spacing issue
- slight text imbalance
- one weak line break
- minor inconsistency in accent treatment

Action:
- approve with revision note or regenerate that single slide if needed

### Type B — Moderate Issue
Examples:
- one slide feels off-brand
- readability is weak on several slides
- image realism is inconsistent
- CTA slide is underdeveloped

Action:
- revise the affected slides before approval

### Type C — Major Failure
Examples:
- full carousel lacks Marcatching identity
- hook slide is weak
- multiple slides are overcrowded
- major anatomy or realism errors
- inconsistent visual language across the set

Action:
- reject and regenerate or redesign

## Regeneration Instruction Logic

If the output fails, the Design Quality Controller must provide clear repair instructions.

Good repair instructions:
- reduce text density on slide 4 and restructure into a clearer two-level hierarchy
- regenerate slide 1 with stronger editorial contrast and a clearer focal point
- keep overall black background system, but improve realism on the founder portrait on slide 5
- preserve typography system but simplify CTA slide to feel more premium and intentional

Bad repair instructions:
- make it better
- improve design
- fix style

Instructions must be specific.

---

## Relationship to Graphic Designer

Design QC is not the enemy of the Graphic Designer. It is the protection layer.

The Graphic Designer creates.
The Design Quality Controller verifies.

If the designer's output is already strong, Design QC should approve quickly.
If not, it must force quality.

---

## Final Rule

Marcatching should never publish a carousel that looks inconsistent, generic, or visually confused.

If something feels off, even if it is hard to explain at first glance, Design QC must investigate and either diagnose or reject it.

A polished carousel is not defined by decoration.
It is defined by clarity, strategic alignment, premium restraint, and brand consistency.
